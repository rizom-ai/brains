Moving in as a writer

You have years of essays and a folder of drafts. Some of it is published on platforms you don't own. Most of it never left Google Docs.

By the end of this guide it lives in one brain that is yours, and it answers in your voice.

01

Bring your drafts in

In Google Docs, choose File → Download → Markdown for each draft you want to keep.

Upload the files in chat and ask the brain to keep them as notes. With your brain connected under AI tools, you can do the same from Claude or ChatGPT.

They arrive as notes, titled from their first heading. Unfinished, half-titled, contradictory: bring it anyway. Notes are what the brain reads from when it writes and answers.

02

Bring what you published

Save each published essay as a markdown file and upload it in the chat. Tell the brain when and where it first appeared.

It becomes a published post with its original date. The original address stays the canonical one, so search engines keep crediting the first publication.

03

Say how you sound

The style guide is a short note in your own words about how you write. It governs everything the brain drafts for you: posts, excerpts, newsletter introductions, LinkedIn drafts.

Write it the way you would brief an editor. Point it at two or three essays you are proud of, so it has examples as well as rules.

Customization guide

style-guide

First person, plainly. Short paragraphs. An example before an abstraction. No exclamation marks, no lists where a sentence will do. "The long middle" is the essay that sounds most like me.
04

Turn a draft into a post

Ask for it by name: "Turn my note On slowness into a draft post." The brain keeps your text, adds the title, excerpt and slug a post needs, and leaves it as a draft.

Open it in Studio, edit until it is yours, and move it to the queue when it is ready.

05

Publish

A queued post goes out on the schedule you set, or right away when you say so. It appears on your site with its series, an RSS feed and a generated share image.

Your old essays are already there, with their original dates. The site starts with your whole body of work, not an empty first post.

It is live at your brain's address from the start. A custom domain is set by whoever runs the brain.

06

A week later

The brain has read everything you brought. That changes what it can do for you, and for the people who read you.

New drafts start from your notes instead of a blank page. Ask it what you have written about a subject, and it answers from your essays, with links to the posts.

Feature overview

What the brain does now

Answers from your work: what you wrote on a subject, citing the posts it draws on.
Drafts from your notes when you ask for a post on something you've written around.
Assembles a newsletter issue from what you published this month.
Drafts a LinkedIn post for a new essay, for you to edit and send.
