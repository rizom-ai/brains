Studio, arranged around what you come to do

These mockups go with the overhaul plan. Studio is currently laid out the way the brain is built: entity types, plugin workspaces, frontmatter fields. Here it is laid out around five jobs: see what needs you, write, publish, ask, and tend the brain.

One idea runs through all of them. Publishing is a path of lights (draft, queued, published), and it appears in the same form in the sidebar, the editor header and the Publish board. Content comes from the publishing test app; the attention items are illustrative.

Home

Replaces Overview and its event log.

  • A sentence says how much needs you, so a calm day reads as calm.
  • Needs you lists only actionable items, each with the verb that clears it.
  • One box captures a note or asks the brain, without choosing a type first.
  • Continue brings back unfinished drafts and conversations.
  • System health is one line; the full history moves to Brain → History.
  • One sidebar with five sections replaces the rail, the leaf column and the ordinals.

Board, under Home

Shows how a plugin page finds its place.

  • action-board declares section: "today", so its board sits under Home next to Inbox. Studio has no code for it.
  • The board is a declarative block. Publish uses the same block with its own columns.
  • The buttons on a card are action-board's own transitions (Start, Block, Done, Reopen). Dragging runs the same actions.
  • Cards keep where the item came from: a team conversation, the inbox or a chat.
  • Blocked items also reach Home's Needs you as attention items.

Writing

Replaces the four-column editor and the status select.

  • Opening an entry folds the sidebar to a rail; the manuscript gets the center.
  • The title is the document heading and is edited in place. There's no Title field.
  • The header shows where the post is on its path, and Publish is the action that moves it.
  • Fields needed only for publishing are marked where they live, not required up front.
  • Properties are an inspector that ⌘\ hides. Address and sync stay folded.
  • Source shows live formatting, with heading marks in the margin; Preview is one key away.

Post in Urban systems

Urban Sensing Networks and the Data Commons

Cities are filling up with sensors: air quality on lampposts, footfall counters at station exits, noise meters on school roofs. Almost none of that data is shared with the people who live next to the sensors.

The usual reason given is privacy. The real reason is that nobody designed for sharing, so the default became hoarding.

What a commons needs

A data commons is not an open-data portal. It needs three things a portal doesn't have: a verifiable record of where each reading came from, rules the community can change, and someone accountable for upkeep.

If residents can't trace a reading back to its sensor, they have no reason to trust the dashboard built on top of it.

The first of these is the one I've written about before, and it's the cheapest to get right

Asking about a selection

Replaces the rewrite bar above the body, and its Model / brain picker.

  • Select text and press Ask (⌘J): the panel is about the selection. Clearing it goes back to the whole post.
  • Who to ask sits in the panel: your brain, or a brain you are connected to. A connected brain answers from what it has published.
  • Review, Fact-check, Related and Rewrite are starters, as the bar's presets were.
  • A rewrite comes back as a proposal: Replace selection puts it in the draft, and nothing is saved until you save.
