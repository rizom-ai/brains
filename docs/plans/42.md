# Plan: 42 — a brain for 42workspace

## Status

Planned. Ohad Gilad, owner of 42workspace in Rotterdam, asked for a brains demo to his team on Tuesday 3 November 2026. The goal of the demo is a pilot: 42workspace runs its own brain, and offers a brain to each of its members. Nothing is deployed yet. The fleet brain `42` is built and checked between Saturday 31 October and Monday 2 November, and stays unlisted until Ohad agrees to the pilot.

## Goal

A fleet brain `42` at `42.rizom.ai` holding 42workspace's own knowledge, seeded from its public site [42workspace.com](https://42workspace.com), and a live demo in which 42's staff use it as their shared memory and a member's brain asks it questions.

| Brain    | Holder                      | Domain        | Content repo                |
| -------- | --------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- |
| `42`     | 42workspace                 | `42.rizom.ai` | `rizom-ai/rover-42-content` |
| (yeehaa) | Jan Hein, as example member | `yeehaa.io`   | existing, unchanged         |

## Non-goals

- No custom 42 site package or theme before Tuesday. A site in 42's own look is the first deliverable of the pilot once Ohad agrees.
- No brains for real 42 members before the pilot is agreed, and no import of the members directory: those are people who have not consented to a brain. The example member is the maintainer's own brain.
- No `agent_connect` between `42` and any other brain before Tuesday, and so no `network_ask` in the demo (see Decisions).
- No guest Public Ask, no Discord, no publishing bundle.
- No changes to the brains runtime. Everything here is fleet configuration and content.

## Decisions

### An organization brain with the team bundle

42's brain is `profileKind: organization` and `anchorKind: organization`, with the `team` bundle turned on. 42workspace is a community hub, not a team: the anchor profile is the organization's, and an organization anchor makes the brain collectively owned, so admins can change without losing the brain. The team bundle gives Ohad's staff what they use daily — conversation memory and action cards at `shared` visibility, and trusted users allowed to create notes, links, images, docs and decisions — so the answers, decisions and follow-ups of the community team stay with 42 instead of with one person.

Kind and bundles are independent: brains-ops only validates the `profileKind` and `anchorKind` enums, and the resolver does no kind–bundle cross-check. No fleet brain runs this combination yet, and no eval suite covers it, which is why Phase 2 boots it locally before anything reaches rover-pilot.

The cohort follows `cohorts/rizom-team.yaml`, the fleet's one team brain, and adds `site`: bundles `core, media, web, chat, site, team`, with `addOverride: [docs, action-board]`, since neither `docs` nor `action-board` is a member of any bundle.

### Who sees what

The permission lattice maps onto 42 directly. The maintainer is the first `admin`, through the setup email, and Ohad becomes `admin` when the pilot starts. Staff are `trusted`, invited by an admin from the People workspace with a passkey setup link. Members are `public`, and need no account: invitations only grant `admin` or `trusted`, and the team bundle makes `mcp:http` and web chat admin-only.

Members therefore meet 42's brain in two ways: the public site, and their own brains. A member's brain asks 42's brain over A2A, and 42 answers from its `public` content within the public-ask allowance (`plugins.a2a.publicAsks`). That is the shape of the pilot offer — a brain per member, connected to 42's — rather than a login to 42's.

### Generic site, unlisted

The site is the built-in `@brains/site-organization` with `@brains/theme-rizom`, as `packages/brain-cli/test-apps/team/brain.yaml` uses. Built-in `@brains/*` packages take no version pins in `siteOverride` and add no image pins. The site renders `/` from the anchor profile's `tagline` and `intro`, `/about` from the organization profile fields, and entity pages for `agent`, `topic` and `link`.

The site-builder has no noindex option, and production builds serve `Allow: /`. Until Ohad agrees, `users/42.yaml` carries `plugins.site-builder` settings that keep it out of search engines: a static asset `/robots.txt` with `User-agent: *` / `Disallow: /`, which replaces the generated one, and `headScripts` with `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`, injected into every page head. The agent card and `sitemap.xml` are still served; nothing links to them.

### One-shot calls, not connections

`agent_connect` saves the peer as an `agent` entity with `public` visibility. Every approved public agent is listed in the brain's `/.well-known/agent-directory.json`, peers' daily `directory-scan` picks it up, and the organization homepage's radar draws it. There is no private connect. Connecting `yeehaa.io` and `42.rizom.ai` would advertise 42 before Ohad has agreed.

The demo uses `agent_call` to the exact domain instead. For an unsaved domain it makes a one-shot call after verifying the agent card over HTTPS, and saves nothing. Inbound calls from an unconnected brain are answered at `public` level, which is what a member's brain would get. `network_ask`, which only asks approved peers, waits for the pilot.

### Content from 42workspace.com

`brains-ops onboard` seeds only `anchor-profile/anchor-profile.md`, with `name`, `description`, `website`, `email`, `socialLinks` and the story; `users/<handle>/content/` is a mirror of what it seeded, not an input. The rest of the content is committed straight to `rizom-ai/rover-42-content`, all at `public` visibility:

- `anchor-profile/anchor-profile.md` — completed with the organization fields `tagline`, `intro`, `mission`, `audience`, `focusAreas`, `offerings`, `values`, from the site's home, story and tech-hub pages
- `site-info/site-info.md` — title, description, call to action
- `doc` entries — memberships and spaces (day pass to private office), meeting rooms and venues (rooftop, Sky-Floor, Cyberworkplace), community and events, guides, and practical information; there is no `event` entity type, so recurring events are described in a doc
- `faq` entries — from the site's FAQ page
- `link` entries — contact, jobs and the Dutch site

Text is taken from 42's own pages and attributed to them; nothing is invented. The content is staged in a local directory first, used for Phase 2, and committed to the content repo in Phase 4.

## Demo

The demo leads with the community, not the technology. A space like 42 runs on what a few people know: who the members are, what happened at last month's events, the answers the community team gives every day. Heroics are not infrastructure. The brain lets 42 carry its own memory, and lets members bring theirs.

1. **42's brain.** The site at `42.rizom.ai`, then chat as staff: questions the team gets every day — memberships, rooms, the rooftop, events — answered from 42's own content.
2. **Shared memory.** A decision taken in one staff conversation is found by another; a follow-up lands on the action board.
3. **A member's brain.** From `yeehaa.io`, `agent_call` to `42.rizom.ai` with a member's question, answered from 42's public content. Then the other way: 42's brain asks `yeehaa.io` about the member's work.
4. **The pilot.** One brain for 42, a brain for each member who wants one, how a member gets theirs (`brains-ops user:add` and `onboard` in a 42 cohort), and 42's own site as the first deliverable.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Content (Saturday)

1. Read every page listed on 42workspace.com (memberships, meeting rooms and venues, community, events, tech hub, guides, updates, team, FAQ, contact) and write the entries listed under Decisions into a local content directory.
2. Validate each entry against its entity schema by booting it in Phase 2; fix any that fail rather than dropping them.

### Phase 2 — Local pre-check (Saturday)

1. Write a scratch `brain.yaml` matching the fleet config: `kind` and `anchor` `organization`, the cohort's bundles and additions, the site and theme, the `plugins.site-builder` noindex settings, and `directory-sync` with `seedContentPath` pointing at the Phase 1 content.
2. Build `packages/brain-cli` once and boot the scratch app from its directory with `INIT_CWD="$PWD" bun --no-orphans <repo>/packages/brain-cli/dist/brain.js start`.
3. Trigger a site rebuild on the running app with `brain --remote http://localhost:8080 build-site preview`, as an admin, and inspect `dist/site-preview`; then build production and inspect `dist/site-production`.
4. Verify: the brain boots with no configuration errors; conversation memory and action cards resolve to `shared`; `/` and `/about` show 42's profile; `robots.txt` is `Disallow: /` and every page carries the noindex meta in both builds; chat questions from the demo are answered from the seeded content.

### Phase 3 — Fleet PR (Saturday)

On a branch in rover-pilot, from current `origin/main`:

1. `bun install`, then `bunx brains-ops user:add . 42 --cohort 42`.
2. `cohorts/42.yaml`: `brainVersionOverride` set to the current release, `bundlesOverride: [core, media, web, chat, site, team]`, `addOverride: [docs, action-board]`.
3. `users/42.yaml`: `profileKind` and `anchorKind` `organization`; `anchorProfile` with name 42workspace, description and website; `discord.enabled: false` with no `anchorUserId`; `playbooks.onboarding: false`; `setup` by email to `yeehaa@rizom.ai`; `siteOverride` with the site and theme; `plugins.site-builder` with the noindex settings.
4. `bunx brains-ops render .`, `bunx brains-ops secrets:encrypt . 42`, commit, open a draft PR.
5. Check the PR's Capability Change Report shows `42` with exactly the cohort's bundles and additions.

### Phase 4 — Deploy (Sunday)

Only after the maintainer merges the Phase 3 PR.

1. `bunx brains-ops onboard . 42`: creates `rizom-ai/rover-42-content` and seeds the anchor profile.
2. Commit the Phase 1 content to the content repo; directory-sync pulls it.
3. Verify `https://42.rizom.ai/health/ready` is 200, `https://42.rizom.ai/robots.txt` is `Disallow: /`, and the home page carries the noindex meta.
4. Sign in as admin through the setup email's passkey link, rebuild the production site through the running brain, and check `/` and `/about`.

### Phase 5 — Brain to brain (Sunday)

1. Check that `yeehaa.io` (brain `0.2.0-alpha.590`) supports a one-shot `agent_call` to an unsaved domain; if it does not, upgrading `yeehaa.io` to the release `42` runs becomes part of this phase.
2. From `yeehaa.io`, `agent_call` `42.rizom.ai` with a demo question; from `42`, `agent_call` `yeehaa.io`.
3. Verify both answers, and that neither brain has a new `agent` entity and neither `/.well-known/agent-directory.json` lists the other.

### Phase 6 — Dry run (Monday)

1. Walk the demo end to end on the live brains, with a staff conversation, a decision, an action card and both calls.
2. Fix what breaks; leave the brain's state clean for Tuesday.

## Validation

- Phase 2 local checks pass before the fleet PR is opened.
- The Capability Change Report matches the cohort.
- `42.rizom.ai` is healthy, unlisted (`robots.txt` and noindex meta), and shows 42's profile.
- Both one-shot calls answer, and no directory lists `42`.
- The dry run completes without a manual fix during the walk-through.

## After Tuesday

If Ohad agrees to the pilot, unlisting ends and the pilot starts: remove the noindex settings, connect brains with `agent_connect` and set trust levels, invite 42's staff as `trusted`, add members' brains to a 42 cohort with `user:add` and `onboard`, and build 42's own site package and theme. That work gets its own plan.

## Risks

- **Organization with team is untested.** Mitigated by the Phase 2 boot before any fleet change.
- **`yeehaa.io` runs an older release.** Its one-shot call behaviour is checked in Phase 5, with an upgrade as the fallback.
- **The timeline is three days.** Each phase is small and checked on its own; the generic site keeps the critical path to configuration and content.
