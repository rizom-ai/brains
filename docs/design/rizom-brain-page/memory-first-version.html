01 · Capture

Start with the work you already have.

A project folder. A decision log. The notes you keep meaning to turn into something. Give your brain a useful starting point, not another empty workspace.

Read and edit the work in Studio. Keep the underlying markdown in your own editor or Git workflow.

You don’t need to organise your whole life before the first useful question.

02 · Ask

Less explaining. More thinking.

Ask a question against your own work. Follow an answer back to its sources. Use the conversation to find a connection, test an idea, or move a draft forward.

A conversation with your material · example

Why did we decide to start with one team?

The early conversations pointed to repeated explanations inside one team, rather than a lack of information across the organisation.1 A smaller pilot lets you test whether the brain reduces that repetition before involving more people.2

The decision was to use existing project notes and review the experience after four weeks.3

04 · Connect

Share a thought. Not your whole brain.

Publish selected work to your site. Connect your tools through MCP. Publish an agent card when you want your brain to be discoverable.

These are different doors, not one big “share everything” switch. A connection is not permission to everything.
