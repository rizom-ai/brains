The workshop’s main weakness is that it treats “play” primarily as an individual mindset and short-term experiment. The essay argues for something more structural: redesigning collaboration around collective coordination without control, shared ownership, values-based networks, and technology that removes busywork. As written, participants may leave with personal productivity experiments rather than a shared alternative to industrial-era work.

**Proposal:** Replace the 20–27 minute “Experiment design” segment with a **Collective redesign lab**. In small groups, choose one real collaboration challenge and redesign it using four constraints: voluntary participation, participant-defined rules, freedom to fail, and shared ownership of the resulting value. Have each group specify what technology or process would remove administrative friction, how decisions would be made without hierarchy, and what commitment they would test over two weeks.

This would preserve the workshop’s practical ending while making the essay’s central claim tangible: play is not simply feeling better about existing work; it is changing how people coordinate and create value together.
