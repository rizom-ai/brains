Asking on a phone

On desktop the conversation sits beside the map. A phone has no room for that. The box sits halfway down, the keyboard covers it, the answer grows out of view, and the map zooms to its sources off screen. On a phone, asking therefore opens the conversation full screen. A compact strip of the map stays on top, so the answer's sources still light up where you can see them.

2 · Typing, keyboard open

The conversation takes the whole screen, and the page underneath stops scrolling. While the keyboard is open, the sheet fits the space above it.

  • The map strip folds away, so the conversation keeps the room a small phone has left.
  • The composer sits directly on the keyboard and follows it as it opens and closes.
  • Sending scrolls your question into view and closes the keyboard, so the answer and the map get the screen back.
  • ✕, the phone's back gesture and Escape all close the conversation.

4 · Closing keeps your place

Closing the conversation drops you back on the page where you left it, and the answer's sources stay lit on the map.

  • A pill brings the same conversation back.
  • A reload starts fresh, as it does today.

What changes, and where

  • Only on phones (below 48rem). The desktop atlas keeps its side-by-side conversation.
  • Web Chat's Ask box gets a full-screen sheet mode. It sizes itself to the visual viewport, so the composer rides on the keyboard. It locks the page scroll, uses a history entry so Back closes it, scrolls to your question and closes the keyboard on send.
  • The atlas passes its map into the sheet as a compact strip, using the zoom it already has for cited sources. The strip folds away while the keyboard is open, and the pill shows after closing.
