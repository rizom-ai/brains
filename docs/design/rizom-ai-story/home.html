Distributed Living Memory

For hybrid human–AI teams: memory kept in brains each team owns, connected from one team to an economy.

The science

A team’s intelligence is not the sum of its members’ talent.

Organisational psychology has one finding it keeps replicating. Intelligence lives in the team’s transactive memory system — the shared, mostly invisible map of who knows what, whose judgment settles which questions, and how knowledge moves. Strong-memory teams outperform equally talented ones, and the gap is structural rather than personal.

Who knows what
And who is the only one who knows it — the expertise that leaves when they do.
Whose judgment settles it
Which questions end with which person, without anyone calling a meeting.
How knowledge moves
What reaches the right person before they have to think to ask for it.

The shift

Four decades of that research studied teams of humans. Those teams no longer exist.

Every team is now a hybrid team, and AI arrives with no place in the team’s memory — it doesn’t know who knows what, whose word is trusted, or why last year’s decision was made. This is why AI rollouts underdeliver so predictably.

The network

Where this goes

Measure. Connect. Coordinate.

A score read from the work, not a survey In development
Forty years of research assessed transactive memory by questionnaire — self-reported, twice a year, out of date by the time it is read. The same three dimensions can be derived from what a team actually does: continuous, behavioural, and a number an organisation can move.
Brains that reach each other — signed, permissioned, selective Emerging
Each brain keeps its own boundary and shares only what it chooses to, so a team’s memory can reach an organisation and an organisation’s can reach an ecosystem without anyone handing over the whole map.
An economy’s coordination layer Not yet built
A brain holds not just what its people know but how they work. Those same signals become composition logic: teams of people and agents assembled on evidence of how they actually collaborate, rather than on resumes and keywords.
