Rizom Work

Heroics are not infrastructure.

Most organisations run on what a few people know. It sits in their heads, their inboxes and their habits, and it leaks every time a project closes, a team changes or someone leaves.

For hubs For teams and organisations

What we set up

A Brain that holds what a community or a team knows, and keeps it when people move on.

For hubs

A Brain for the hub, and one for every member who wants it

The hub's Brain holds who is there, what they know, which programmes ran and which introductions worked. Members feed it by talking to it in the channels they already use, so the record grows out of the work instead of out of reports.

 For teams and organisations

A team Brain, starting with a setup

A Brain that holds what the organisation knows, why it decided what it did and whom to ask. It starts with a setup at a fixed price, then runs on a monthly fee, or the organisation runs it itself.

For hubs

Hubs lose most of what passes through them.

A programme ends and its lessons leave with the cohort, staff move on with the introductions that worked, and the evidence a funder asks for has to be written up afterwards.

  1. A sponsor and a Brain for the hub. The hub itself, a network of hubs or a funder pays for the hub's Brain, seeded from what the hub already keeps: its programmes, its members and the introductions that worked.
  2. Members use it from the first week. They ask it who knows what and tell it what happened, in the chat they already use, so the hub's memory grows without anyone writing a report.
  3. Members take a Brain of their own. A professional Brain for an independent, a team Brain for a startup or company in the hub: their memory, their site and an agent that works for them, theirs wherever they go next.
  4. Work routed through the network. Service partners, from grant advisers to certified partners, offer their work to members through the network: 25% to the commons, a share to the hub that brought the work in, the rest to whoever delivers.

For teams and organisations

Every team Brain starts with a setup.

The first weeks map where the organisation's knowledge sits, who knows what and where it leaks, and seed the Brain with what that turns up.

At the end of the setup, the organisation chooses

Continue

A monthly fee for hosting and upkeep.

Run it yourself

The software is free and the memory is in open files. Self-hosting docs

Stop

Keep what the setup produced.

Prices for the hub Brain, member Brains, the setup and the monthly fee. Not set yet; a B.V. decision.

Public bodies

Resilience is whether the work carries on when the people who know how it runs are gone.

A body that depends on a few indispensable people, or on one vendor's platform, is fragile. A team Brain it owns, runs on a small open model and can move elsewhere removes both dependencies.

Public innovation programmes put a government body forward as launching customer for a challenge it has set. A public body that runs its own memory is a reference other public bodies listen to.

Why now

The first thing AI runs into is everything nobody wrote down.

An agent cannot ask the colleague who has always known how a decision gets made, so the knowledge that used to live in a few people's heads becomes the bottleneck the moment work is handed to software. Heroics are not infrastructure, and AI makes that visible faster than any reorganisation did.
