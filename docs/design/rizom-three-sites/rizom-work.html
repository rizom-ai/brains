    Rizom Work

    Heroics are not infrastructure.

    Most organizations run on what a few people know. It sits in their heads, their inboxes and their habits, and it leaks every time a project closes, a team changes or someone leaves.

    For hubs →For teams and organizationsThe essay behind it

    The science

    A team's intelligence is not the sum of its members' talent.

    Organizational psychology has one finding it keeps replicating. Intelligence lives in the team's transactive memory system — the shared, mostly invisible map of who knows what, whose judgment settles which questions, and how knowledge moves. Strong-memory teams outperform equally talented ones, and the gap is structural rather than personal.

    Specialization
    Who knows what, and who is the only one who knows it — the expertise that leaves when they do.
    Credibility
    Which questions end with which person, without anyone calling a meeting.
    Coordination
    What reaches the right person before they have to think to ask for it.

    Why now

    The first thing AI runs into is everything nobody wrote down.

    An agent cannot ask the colleague who has always known how a decision gets made, so the knowledge that used to live in a few people's heads becomes the bottleneck the moment work is handed to software. AI makes that visible faster than any reorganization did.

    For hubs

    A Brain for the hub, and one for every member who wants it.

    Hubs lose most of what passes through them. A program ends and its lessons leave with the cohort, staff move on with the introductions that worked, and the evidence a funder asks for has to be written up afterwards.

    1. A sponsor and a Brain for the hub.The hub itself, a network of hubs or a funder pays for the hub's Brain, seeded from what the hub already keeps: its programs, its members and the introductions that worked.
    2. Members use it from the first week.They ask it who knows what and tell it what happened, in the chat they already use, so the hub's memory grows without anyone writing a report.
    3. Members take a Brain of their own.A professional Brain for an independent, a team Brain for a startup or company in the hub: their memory, their site and an agent that works for them, theirs wherever they go next.
    4. Work routed through the network.Service partners offer their work to members through the network: 25% to the commons, a share to the hub that brought the work in, the rest to whoever delivers.

    For teams and organizations

    Every team Brain starts with a setup.

    A team Brain holds what the organization knows, why it decided what it did and whom to ask, and keeps that knowledge when people move on.

    1. A setup at a fixed price.The first weeks map where the organization's knowledge sits, who knows what and where it leaks, and seed the Brain with what that turns up.
    2. Then the organization chooses.Continue on a monthly fee for hosting and upkeep, run the Brain itself, or stop and keep what the setup produced.

    Public bodies

    Resilience is whether the work carries on when the people who know are gone.

    A body that depends on a few indispensable people, or on one vendor's platform, is fragile. A team Brain it owns, runs on a small open model and can move elsewhere removes both dependencies.

    Public innovation programs put a government body forward as launching customer for a challenge it has set.

    Partners

    Work through a partner, or become one.

    Advisers
    Consultancies and grant advisers bring their clients in and stay involved in the setup. A setup can often be funded from the subsidies they apply for.
    Certified partners
    Practitioners certified by the Foundation deliver setups on the standard split: a share to whoever brought the organization in, a share to whoever does the work, and 25% to the commons.
    Who is listed as the team, and in which role. Client references only with the client's consent.
