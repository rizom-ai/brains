    Rizom Foundation

    The case for collective intelligence.

    A world in which intelligence is collective. People and organizations hold what they know, and why, so nothing depends on one indispensable person or one platform.

    Become a founding supporter →Read Common Sense

    What stands in the way

    Memory without a home settles wherever it is cheapest to keep.

    People and organizations rarely lose what they know all at once. It drains away, because their memory has no home of its own: who knows what, why a decision was made, whose judgment settles a question. For a long time that meant a few indispensable people, who take it with them when they leave. Now it increasingly means a model owned by one company, which amplifies whatever it finds, confusion included.

    None of this is a law of nature. What is missing is a commons: memory that keeps its reasons, and agreements that no one in the middle owns.

    The change runs through two steps

    Each with a technical and a social side.

    Memory they own 01
    Each person and organization has a Brain: their memory in open files they own, which people and their AI tools can work from. A practice makes writing down reasons a habit. Knowledge stops depending on who is still in the room.
    Connection without a center 02
    An open standard lets independent parties find each other by expertise, share what they choose and keep a record of their agreements, signed by both and held by each. A charter sets how disagreements are settled. The Foundation holds the rules, never the parties' memory, and anyone can host their own and leave.

    The Rizom Foundation works to decenter AI, moving intelligence back to the people and organizations it comes from.

    It gives them memory of their own and connections that no one in the middle owns, and holds that infrastructure as a commons that cannot be sold.

    How it spreads

    Through hubs, certified partners and a think tank that builds.

    Hubs rizom.work
    A hub gets a Brain of its own, fed by its members, and members who want more take a Brain of their own. An organization outside a hub starts with a setup that maps who knows what and why, and leaves with a Brain of its own and the habit of keeping it.
    Certified partners registry
    Practitioners learn the method by apprenticeship and are certified by the Foundation. The method and the name stay with the Foundation, and 25% of every engagement flows back to the commons.
    A think tank that builds writing · events
    The Foundation publishes its research as essays and working software, so an argument can be tried as well as read, and takes part in the debate on digital sovereignty in Europe and Taiwan.

    Five assumptions, tested in public

    Each has a test and its thresholds, published before it runs.

    • A1Memory that people own strengthens their map of who knows what, and keeps it intact when people leave.
    • A2People will hold their own memory once it costs no more effort than handing it over.
    • A3Connection without a center can be made as easy as a platform, so parties do not drift back to one.
    • A4Others can learn the method through apprenticeship, without its author in the room.
    • A5Organizations pay for the method and hosting though the software is free, and a share of that funds the commons.
    Each assumption links to its test and, later, its results. None is published yet.

    Goals for the end of 2027

    What the Foundation has to be able to show.

    Memory they own 3 organizations
    At least three organizations run their own Brain for six months without the founder's hands, with their memory measured before and after. One rehearses the exit to self-hosting.
    Connection without a center 6 months
    The open standard and the charter are published, and two independent parties have kept an agreement through it for six months, with no one from Rizom in the middle.
    Certified partners 2 certified
    The registry is public, at least two partners are certified, and one has delivered without the founder, on par with the founder's own work.
    Hubs half still in use
    The first public program has run in a hub, and six months later at least half of its organizations still use the memory it left behind.
    Research in public 5 of 5
    All five assumptions are published with their tests before they run, and with their results afterwards, including any that fail.
    Funding about €115,000
    The first year is funded from pledges and the commons remit with no say attached, plus a first grant in the Foundation's own name.

    The parts that make memory a commons rather than a product.

    Published record types, so that one Brain can read another without a translator in the middle.
    Standard and charter connection
    How independent Brains find and trust each other, keep a shared record and hold to an agreement, and how disagreements are settled.

    The public list of practitioners certified by the Foundation. It opens with the first certifications.

    Why a foundation

    Infrastructure people depend on should not belong to whoever happens to sell it.

    The Foundation owns the code, the method and the Rizom name. The core stays under AGPL, the SDK and protocols under Apache. Rizom B.V. runs hosting and client work under license, cannot take the commons with it, and pays 25% of every engagement back. An independent board majority decides on the license, the name and the company, and the Foundation takes no investors.
