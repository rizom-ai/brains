## Bring your drafts in

In Google Docs, choose **File → Download → Markdown** for each draft you want to keep.

Upload a file in chat and ask the brain to keep it as a note. It asks you to confirm before it saves anything, and it saves one file per request: when you attach several, ask for the next one after each save. With your brain connected under **AI tools**, you can do the same from Claude or ChatGPT.

They arrive as **notes**. Unfinished, half-titled, contradictory: bring it anyway. Notes are what the brain reads from when it writes and answers.

Exact steps: [Interface setup](../interface-setup.md), [Content management](../content-management.md).

## Bring what you published

Save each published essay as a markdown file and upload it in chat. Tell the brain when and where it first appeared.

It becomes a **published post** with its original date. The original address stays the canonical one, so search engines keep crediting the first publication.

Exact steps: [Entity types](../entity-types-reference.md).

## Say how you sound

The **style guide** is a short note in your own words about how you write. It governs everything the brain drafts for you: posts, excerpts, newsletter introductions, LinkedIn drafts.

Write it the way you would brief an editor. Point it at two or three essays you are proud of, so it has examples as well as rules.

Exact steps: [Customization guide](../customization-guide.md).

## Turn a draft into a post

Ask for it by name: _"Turn my note On slowness into a draft post."_ The brain keeps your text, adds the title, excerpt and slug a post needs, and leaves it as a **draft**.

Open it in Studio, edit until it is yours, and move it to the queue when it is ready.

Exact steps: [Content management](../content-management.md).

## Publish

A queued post goes out on the schedule you set, or right away when you say so. It appears on your site with its series, an RSS feed and a generated share image.

Your old essays are already there, with their original dates. The site starts with your whole body of work, not an empty first post. It is live at your brain's address from the start; a custom domain is set by whoever runs the brain.

Exact steps: [Content management](../content-management.md).

## A week later

The brain has read everything you brought. New drafts start from your notes instead of a blank page. Ask it what you have written about a subject, and it answers from your essays, with links to the posts.

- **Answers from your work:** what you wrote on a subject, citing the posts it draws on.
- **Drafts from your notes** when you ask for a post on something you have written around.
- **Assembles a newsletter issue** from what you published this month.
- **Drafts a LinkedIn post** for a new essay, for you to edit and send.

Exact steps: [Feature overview](../feature-overview.md).
